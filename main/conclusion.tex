\chapter{Conclusion and Open Questions}
\label{chap:conclusion}

\section{Summary}
\label{sec:concl-summary}

This thesis brought automated, contract-based leak discovery to AArch64 and used it to find and
weaponize a new class of Arm leaks. We ported \revizor{}'s model-based relational testing to
AArch64 with a native-execution contract executor, added headless fuzzing of remote cores, and
modeled the Pointer Authentication and Memory Tagging extensions
(\autoref{chap:port}). We showed that \revizor{}'s priming step is incomplete (it cannot report a
leak whose only effect on its own run lies outside the L1 data cache), and replaced it with a
generalized rule that strictly generalizes the contract-violation definition, provably detects
strictly more, and localizes each report to a genuine violation with two optimal localizers
(\autoref{chap:priming}). We introduced precondition fuzzing, which seals the precondition sites of
a test case and fills them per input to satisfy or violate a precondition on a speculative-only
path, without ever faulting the victim (\autoref{chap:precond}). Together these uncovered a leak
rooted in the CPU's canonicality and alignment checks on loads, stores, and indirect branches
(\autoref{chap:canon}), which reproduces across server-class and mobile Arm cores and crosses the
process, virtualization, and privilege boundaries, and which we weaponized end to end to recover a
complete RSA-2048 private key through OpenSSL (\autoref{chap:eval}).

\section{Open questions}
\label{sec:concl-open}

\paragraph{The unexplained prefetcher effect.}
The L1D instruction-pointer prefetcher violation observed on the Alder Lake baseline is
deterministic in the prefetcher-control bit but not yet root-caused. Explaining it, and
determining whether an analogous effect exists on the Arm targets, is left to future work.

\paragraph{The secure / non-secure boundary.}
A necessary precondition for crossing the secure boundary holds on our hardware: an EL3 world
switch does not by itself flush the normal-world branch-target buffer, so the BTB eviction channel
survives it. Whether the channel actually crosses to a secure-world victim, which requires a
trusted-execution vehicle we did not have, remains open.
